## 1. Narrative

**Central question:** *Can one line change how a country moves?*

The story moves from **what exists** to **what is promised**, and keeps the two apart throughout:

1. **Hook.** An electric train crosses the savanna at golden hour. Tanzania is building a new railway across the country.
2. **Scale.** The map shows the corridor following the 1914 metre-gauge Central Line. Dar es Salaam–Dodoma operates; the line on to Mwanza (1,219 km of phase one) and the Tabora–Kigoma branch are under construction.
3. **Engineering.** The built section station by station, then the wider 1,435 mm gauge and track layers, the 25 kV electric power and design speeds, and the fleet.
4. **What changes.** Journey time against the bus, and passenger journeys. Measured freight sits next to the projection, and it falls far short. Passengers are on record; the freight case is still being made.
5. **Bigger picture.** The line starts at the port and is built to reach Lake Victoria and Lake Tanganyika. The Burundi link has been launched; Rwanda is proposed. Each is labelled with its real status, and the one projected benefit shown carries a PROJECTED tag.
6. **Close.** A train approaches at blue hour. *The electric line reaches Dodoma. Mwanza, Kigoma and Burundi are still being built. Its real measure will be what it carries.*

**Deliberately left out (unverified or contradictory):**
- Total network length (2,561 km and 2,809 km are both reported).
- Total project cost.
- A Dar es Salaam–Mwanza opening date (2027 and 2028 are both cited).
- Tunnel and bridge counts.
- The "over seven million passengers" figure (undated).
- The 22.4% growth figure (unclear whether it is SGR only).
- TRC "profitability" (the figures are income, not profit).
- Fares (2024 figures; no current fare confirmed).
- Train livery: trains are shown backlit in silhouette so that no livery detail is invented.

**Map:** country outlines from Natural Earth 1:50m (world-atlas); lakes from Natural Earth 1:10m (@geo-maps/earth-lakes-10m). Alignments are drawn through station and town positions and smoothed. They are schematic at this scale and captioned as such on screen.

## 4. Design system

- **Colour.**
  - Burnt orange `#E8752A`: SGR lines, key numbers and status chips only.
  - Charcoal `#161615` / near-black `#0B0B0A`: editorial scenes.
  - Cream `#EFE7D6` and map paper `#F4EDDF`/`#E4DCC8`: the map.
  - White: headlines.
  - Greys `#8E8C86` / `#5E5C57`: secondary text and the Central Line.
- **Type.**
  - Archivo (variable, condensed 75%, 800) for headlines and numbers.
  - Archivo normal width, tracked capitals for kickers and labels.
  - Hanken Grotesk for supporting text.
  - These are the website's own self-hosted fonts.
  - Minimum on-screen size is 17 px for map captions only; body text is 22 px or more.
- **Status grammar (consistent across the film).**
  - Operating: solid orange line.
  - Under construction: dashed orange, slowly marching.
  - Proposed: grey dotted.
  - Central Line: fine grey ties.
  - Data chips: MEASURED, REPORTED and TIMETABLE are solid; PROJECTED is a dashed outline.
- **Motion.**
  - Cubic in-out for camera moves; quart-out for reveals.
  - Text rises into a mask.
  - Counters only for verified figures.
  - Lines draw in route order.
  - Every transition is motivated by the subject: the track line opening onto the map, a push into the operating line, or a map fade to the closing landscape.
- **Safe area.** 120 px side margins and 90 px or more top and bottom: inside title-safe at 1080p.
- **Finish.** Animated film grain, a paper texture on maps and a gentle vignette.

## 7. Imagery: what is generated, and the gaps

- **Everything on screen is drawn in code** (`film.js`): landscapes, trains, maps, diagrams and charts. No stock footage, no third-party imagery and no AI-generated images, so there is nothing to license and no risk of inconsistent train designs.
- **Gap: real footage.** Photoreal train footage could not be generated in code or sourced from this production environment, which had no access to image or footage libraries. The two cinematic scenes (0:00–0:16 and 1:45–2:00) are painted, backlit dusk scenes, designed so the train reads as a silhouette rather than a specific livery.
  - **Practical upgrade:** request B-roll from the Tanzania Railways Corporation (TRC) communications unit, or license it from an agency.
  - Wanted: a side-on tracking shot of an EMU at golden hour, and a head-on approach at dusk.
  - Lay it into those two windows. The text layers need no change, because they sit in the sky area of the frame.
- **Map alignments** are schematic. For a survey-accurate line, replace `routes` in `build-geo.mjs` with TRC or OpenStreetMap geometry.

## 8. How to rebuild

```bash
npm ci
# map data (needs the Natural Earth lakes file from npm: npm pack @geo-maps/earth-lakes-10m)
node motion/sgr/build-geo.mjs path/to/package/map.geo.json
# preview in a browser with a scrubber (space = play/pause)
node motion/sgr/render.mjs --serve      # → http://127.0.0.1:4400/motion/sgr/film.html?preview
# stills, or the full silent master (~10 min on 4 cores)
node motion/sgr/render.mjs --stills 5,20,64
node motion/sgr/render.mjs --video --out motion/sgr/out/sgr-master-silent.mp4
# score, then mux and encode the web versions
python3 motion/sgr/audio.py
bash motion/sgr/encode.sh
```

All words and numbers on screen are in `content.js`. Edit them there and re-render.
